§01

The company, in plain terms

Taxfix SE was founded in Berlin in 2016 by Lino Teuteberg and Mathis Büchi on one observation: roughly half of German employees who are owed a refund never claim it, because the Steuererklärung is genuinely frightening. Their answer was to throw away the form entirely and replace it with a chat-style questionnaire — you photograph your Lohnsteuerbescheinigung, answer plain-language questions, and the app does the mapping to tax law behind the curtain.

The stated mission has stayed remarkably consistent: "fix finance for all" — make financial obligations legible to people who were never taught them. Internally the phrasing you'll hear is about confidence and control over your own money, not about tax software. That distinction matters for what you build: they see themselves as a consumer finance company that happens to start at tax, not a tax tool.

Distribution partners — the closest thing they have to enterprise clients

Taxfix's partner model is almost entirely embedded referral inside another company's app, priced as a discount to that company's users:

  • N26 — the flagship. N26 customers get 30% off on Standard, up to 50% off on premium tiers. Taxfix is bundled into N26 Business Metal's perks alongside Fiverr and Dropbox. The partnership runs in Germany and ran in Italy too.
  • Qonto — their first named B2B partnership, reaching European SMEs and freelancers through a business banking product. Strategically the right door for the new self-employed product.
  • Employer benefit deals — e.g. Mymenu in Italy offered Taxfix to all staff including delivery riders as a welfare benefit. This is the shape they want to scale: one contract, thousands of users, near-zero CAC.
  • UK partner programme — an open pitch to any company whose customer base needs personal tax help (they specifically court audiences of self-employed, landlords, investors, expats, construction workers, company directors).
The gap worth pointing at

Every one of those partnerships is a discount link. None of them is an API, an embedded SDK, or a white-label surface. A neobank, a payroll provider, or an HR platform cannot currently build tax into their own product using Taxfix — they can only hand their user off to a different app with a coupon. That is a large, obvious, unbuilt product: Taxfix-as-infrastructure. If your team wants a business-model swing rather than a feature, this is the one.

§05

The three-front squeeze

German business press has spent 2026 writing about Taxfix in explicitly existential terms — one headline literally reads "the tax app Taxfix's fight against AI death." That framing is now internal vocabulary. Three separate forces are compressing the same product at the same time.

Front 01 · Substitution

General-purpose AI

OpenAI reported tax-related ChatGPT queries up 300% in Q1 2026. The question users now ask out loud: why pay €49.99 for guided simplicity when a subscription I already have explains the form for free? Taxfix's original value proposition — guidance through complexity — is now a commodity feature of a chatbot.

Front 02 · Price collapse

Check24 Steuer, free

In Finanztip's 2026 test, Check24's completely free tax app scored 9.49 and finished second overall — ahead of every paid competitor except WISO, and well ahead of Taxfix's 8.3. It's funded by Check24's comparison business, so it never has to make money on tax. Taxfix is the most expensive app in the table.

Front 03 · Disintermediation

The German state

The coalition is rolling out an automatically pre-filled digital tax return on your phone, submittable in one click. Sign-ups opened 31 March 2026; first nationwide user group from mid-2026, with a further simplification law drafted by autumn 2026. The explicit goal is to relieve employees and pensioners of filing at all. That is Taxfix's core use case, delivered by the tax office, for free.

"The next 24 months will determine whether Taxfix becomes a German tech success story or a cautionary tale about AI's industry-destroying velocity." German trade press on Taxfix, 2026

Where the defensibility actually is

Worth having on the tip of your tongue, because it's the argument they're making internally and you'll sound aligned if you make it too:

  1. The submission, not the explanation. ChatGPT can explain Werbungskosten. It cannot authenticate to ELSTER, sign with a certificate, file a legally binding return, and be accountable for it. The regulated last mile is the moat.
  2. Liability and licensure. Germany's Steuerberatungsgesetz restricts who may give tax advice. A general chatbot operates entirely outside that perimeter; Taxfix's Expert Service operates inside it. Trust is the product.
  3. Proprietary outcome data. Ten million filed returns with known outcomes — what was claimed, what the Finanzamt accepted, what it rejected. No foundation model has that. It is the one dataset that could make a German tax model measurably better than a general one.
  4. The cases the state will never automate. Pre-filled returns work for the simple salaried case. They do not work for a freelancer with mixed income, a landlord, or a cross-border expat. The state is going to eat the bottom of Taxfix's market and leave the top.

The specific, named deficiencies

  • It miscalculates at the edges. The reviewers' phrasing: the app "did not calculate perfectly."
  • No Günstigerprüfung for married couples. Taxfix cannot check whether joint or separate assessment produces a better outcome. For a married filer that is potentially hundreds of euros left on the table — and it's table stakes in competing products.
  • Self-employed support stops at the Kleinunternehmer threshold. Cross €25k prior-year turnover and the app stops being able to help you. Their newest strategic segment is also their hardest wall.
  • Checking your Steuerbescheid is limited or expensive. The assessment notice arriving 6–12 weeks later is the moment of maximum anxiety and maximum error — and it's where the product is thinnest.
  • Support falls off a cliff when the Finanzamt asks a question. The most-repeated complaint in reviews: the app is great until the tax office writes back, at which point users feel abandoned mid-process. Response times of "eight messages before a reply" appear in reviews.
  • The refund-vs-bill surprise. Users report being told at the very end that they owe money — after investing the full effort. That's a trust failure and a refund-request generator.

The operational bottleneck: human experts

Expert Service is priced at 20% of refund with a €99.99 minimum, and the promised turnaround is roughly four weeks. It depends on licensed German tax advisors, and Germany cannot supply them: over 72% of tax firms report being unable to find qualified staff, more than half of practising tax advisors are 50 or older, and 2026 is the first year in which more German workers retire than enter the workforce (1.1m out, 780k in). Inference Taxfix cannot grow its highest-revenue product by hiring. It can only grow it by making each expert dramatically more productive. That is the clearest, most defensible automation target in the entire company.

Plus the seasonality problem nobody can engineer away

German filing peaks hard into the 31 July deadline. Demand for expert capacity, support headcount and compute is wildly concentrated; for the rest of the year the app has no reason to be opened. Every strategic move they've made in 2026 — the monthly self-employed subscription, the annual plan, the "financial platform" framing — is an attempt to flatten that curve.

And the stack it all runs on

Public A single React Native codebase for mobile with cross-platform modules for web; Node.js and Go microservices plus Pub/Sub workers on the backend (GCP); migrated from a monolith. ELSTER integration via API for submission, and support for the pre-filled-return data retrieval (Belegabruf) which requires the user to hold an ELSTER certificate. Hiring posts confirm GCP data pipelines, GDPR controls, and dataset preparation for ML. Useful if you want to claim your prototype is realistically integrable.

§07

Inside the transformation — the part most teams will miss

This is your edge. Taxfix isn't vaguely "interested in AI." They have published, in their own job ads, a precise and unusual operating philosophy — and the hackathon is a live performance of it. Quote it back at them.

The "Builder" model

Taxfix is restructuring R&D around people they call Builders. Their own definition: someone with a primary craft at which they're genuinely excellent, but who stretches across product, design and engineering as the situation demands, using AI to cover the gaps — and who is "accountable to outcomes, not job descriptions." They work in pods of 2–3. The ads are blunt about what this replaces: "There's no designer to give you a Figma file. No engineer to hand the spec to."

They are currently hiring AI First Builder (Product Management) and AI First Builder (Design), and the requirements name the tools: "you use Claude, Cursor, Lovable or equivalent regularly" and "builder track record: you've made things with AI, not just written specs."

It's easier than ever to get software to 75%. Excellence lives in the remaining 25% — and in knowing when the AI output is wrong even when it looks right. Taxfix, AI First Builder (Design) job description, 2026

That sentence is the judging criterion. A room full of teams will demo something AI-generated that looks right. The team that demos the 25% — the verification layer, the point where the model is caught being confidently wrong, the detail that only a person who understood the domain would have added — wins on their own stated terms.

How far the AI adoption already goes

  • Weekly AI training days for all staff, company-wide.

Inference Do not pitch "you should use AI." They are past that, and it will read as naive. Pitch a specific, uncomfortable, domain-shaped problem that AI is a credible answer to.

§09

Seven things worth building

Ordered by how directly they hit a documented Taxfix pain. Each is scoped so that three people with Cursor can get a convincing version running in 135 minutes.

IDEA 01 · STRONGEST

The tax expert's co-pilot

Their Expert Service is the highest-revenue, lowest-margin product, and Germany has no spare tax advisors to hire. Build the tool that makes one expert do the work of three: drop in a client's document bundle, and it produces a pre-filled draft return plus a ranked queue of exactly the items a human must decide — each flagged with the reason, the amount at stake, and the source document region highlighted.

Why it wins: it is the only idea here that directly relieves a constraint they demonstrably cannot buy their way out of. And it's a perfect expression of the 25% principle — the AI does the extraction, the human does the judgment, and the product's entire job is routing between them.

IDEA 02

Bescheid-Check — the assessment notice decoder

Six to twelve weeks after filing, a dense multi-page Steuerbescheid arrives. Roughly one in five contains an error in the taxpayer's disfavour, the objection window is one month, and Taxfix's own review of this is documented as "limited or expensive."

Build: photograph the notice → the tool diffs it line by line against what was filed, flags each deviation in plain German and English, states the euro impact, and where a deviation looks wrong, drafts the Einspruch letter with the deadline counted down.

Why it wins: it converts their single most anxious, most-complained-about moment into a reason to open the app — and it's a year-round touchpoint on a once-a-year product. Also the most emotionally legible demo in the room: everyone has felt that envelope.

§10

Winning this specific room

172 people registered. Teams need three or more. You get roughly 2–3 minutes to demo, then awards at 21:40. Prizes are per person: €500 / €300 / €100.

Tactics

  • Assemble your three before 19:00. The organisers do cross-disciplinary matching for solo attendees, but that costs you the first 20 minutes. Arrive at 18:00 for check-in and lock your team during networking.
  • Mirror their pod structure. 2–3 Builders is literally their org design. One person owns the working demo path end to end, one owns the artefacts and data, one owns the narrative and the slide. Nobody "does the backend."
  • Protect the demo path. At 20:45, stop building. Rehearse the exact click sequence twice. Record a fallback screen capture. More hackathon demos die on a live API call than on a missing feature.
  • Open with the pain, in their words. Not "we built an AI tax assistant." Try: "Your most repeated review complaint is that support ends when the Finanzamt writes back. Here's that fixed." You will be the only team that has read their reviews.
  • Show the 25%. Deliberately include one moment where your system catches the model being wrong and escalates. That single beat is worth more than three extra features, because it's their stated definition of excellence.
  • Close on a number. Expert minutes saved per case. Percentage of assessment notices with a caught deviation. Euros recovered per married couple. Judges remember one number.
  • Don't pitch "use AI." They generate 90% of their marketing with AI, run weekly AI training days, and hold their own internal AI hackathons. Assume the audience is further along than you are on adoption, and win on domain insight instead.

Vocabulary that proves you did the work

Lohnsteuerbescheinigung
Annual wage tax statement from your employer. Taxfix's entire onboarding starts with photographing this.
Steuerbescheid
The tax office's assessment notice, arriving 6–12 weeks after filing. One month to file an objection.
Einspruch
The formal objection against an assessment. One-month window, strictly enforced.
ELSTER / ERiC
The state's electronic filing portal and its interface. The regulated last mile no chatbot can cross.
Belegabruf / VaSt
Retrieving the data the tax office already holds about you, to pre-fill the return. Requires an ELSTER certificate.
Werbungskosten
Income-related deductible expenses — the main lever on an employee's refund.
EÜR
Cash-basis profit-and-loss statement. Mandatory for self-employed people not doing double-entry books.
UStVA
The advance VAT return, filed monthly or quarterly. The recurring obligation their subscription is built on.
Kleinunternehmerregelung
§19 UStG small-business VAT exemption. Thresholds since 2025: €25k prior year, €100k current year.
Günstigerprüfung
Comparing two legal treatments to find the cheaper one. Notably absent from Taxfix for married couples.
Steuerberatungsgesetz
The law restricting who may give tax advice in Germany. The regulatory perimeter that protects Taxfix from general chatbots.
XRechnung / ZUGFeRD
Germany's structured e-invoice formats (EN 16931). Receiving them is already mandatory; issuing phases in 2027–2028.

Three questions to ask a Taxfix person during networking

  1. "Where does an expert's time actually go on a case — what's the part you wish you could delete?" Gets you a build target from the person who feels the pain.
  2. "What breaks between Taxfix, Steuerbot and the UK product — is it one platform yet?" Integration debt is where the unglamorous, high-value problems live.
  3. "When the state's pre-filled return goes live, which of your users stops needing you?" A pointed question that shows you understand their year. Whatever they answer names the segment they're defending — build for that.
